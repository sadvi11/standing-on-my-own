Standing on my own

About

I grew up somewhere the rules for women were clear, and I followed most of them for a long time before I noticed I was.

Where I started

I am from a very small village in India. The rules there were not hidden. They were said out loud, often, by people who loved me.

You can't do that. That is for men. Just accept it, don't ask questions. Learn the housework. Your husband will take care of you. You will have to adjust. You will have to sacrifice. Stay quiet.

I was good at studies. So I did both. I did all the housework, and studies came first anyway. I did a B.Tech, then an M.Tech. I worked in defence research, and then as a cloud engineer at Nokia, running mobile network systems for carriers in Canada and the US.

None of that felt like rebellion at the time. It felt like working hard inside the rules and hoping the rules would move.

What I was doing for other women

For four years I worked with an NGO helping women speak for themselves and protect themselves. How to say what they wanted. How to stand up when something was not okay.

I was good at it. I believed every word of it.

I had not applied any of it to myself.

The question I could not answer

Then I moved to a new country. I told my mentor, more than once and with some pride, that I keep everyone happy.

She asked me why.

I had no answer. Not a bad answer, or a defensive one — nothing at all. I had been doing it so long and so completely that I had never once asked what it was for, or what it was costing, or who I would be without it.

That question is where this writing starts.

What I have been doing since

Learning where I end and other people begin. Noticing when I agree to something because I want to, and when I agree because disagreeing feels dangerous. Finding out what I actually want, which turned out to be harder than any exam I have taken.

I am not finished. I do not think this is the sort of thing that finishes.

Why I write this down

Because I know how the shrinking happens. It is not one dramatic moment. It is a hundred small agreements, each one reasonable on its own, until you look up and cannot find yourself in your own life.

And because the way out is not leaving everyone. I love my family. The question was never whether to choose them or myself — it was whether I could have both, and how.

I want other women to reach that question sooner than I did.

What I am not

I am not a therapist, a psychologist or a counsellor, and nothing I write is treatment or clinical advice. I write about patterns I have lived and things I have learned. Where something needs professional help, I will say so.
