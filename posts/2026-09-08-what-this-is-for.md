*Replace this. It is here so the site is not empty and so the second piece is
easier to write than the first — which is the only real function of a first
piece.*

Some things worth putting in your own version:

**What made you start.** Not the tidy reason. The specific moment.

**Who you are writing for.** "Women" is not an audience. A woman at a
particular point in a particular situation is.

**What you are not offering.** You are not a therapist and this is not advice.
Saying it once, plainly, is fairer to everyone and frees you to write honestly.

**How often, and for how long.** A commitment you can actually keep. Daily is
ambitious; three times a week that survives a bad month beats daily that stops
in three weeks.

---

One note on the writing itself, which I would keep.

The strongest thing in your technical writing is that you document being
wrong — the tests that were lying, the comment describing code you had not
written. People trust it because almost nobody does it.

The same instinct is worth more here, not less. Writing about emotional
independence from a position of having it sorted is the version nobody
believes. Writing from inside the working-out is the version people send to a
friend.
